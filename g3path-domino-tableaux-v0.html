Domino tableaux ↔ shortest paths in the Schreier graph of 3-cycles

An analogue of the Young tableau picture: in G₂ (adjacent transpositions) a shortest path X → Y is a standard Young tableau of shape R(X,Y). In G₃ (cyclic shifts of three adjacent letters) each move places a numbered domino or a single square, and shortest paths correspond to numbered tilings of R by d = |R| − ν(R) pieces.

What is shown here

Graph. Vertices are binary words of length n with k zeros. An edge is a cyclic shift of three adjacent letters (the action of the 3-cycle (i, i+1, i+2)). This is the Schreier graph of Sn over Sk×Sn−k with 3-cycle generators.

Shape. ai = number of 1s to the left of the i-th zero; column i has height ai. If a(X) ≤ a(Y) componentwise, R(X,Y) is the skew shape between the two staircases (red dashed = X, blue = Y).

Moves. The shift 011 ↔ 110 changes one column by 2 (vertical domino), 001 ↔ 100 changes two adjacent columns by 1 (horizontal domino), and the other shifts change a single square.

Domino tableau. A tiling of R by d pieces (dominoes and single squares), numbered 1, …, d, such that after each step the filled part is again a staircase: cell (i, r) is placed only after the cell below it and the cell to its right (when those are in R). The minimum number of pieces is d = |R| − ν(R), where ν(R) is the maximum number of non-overlapping dominoes in R.

Bijection. Path ↦ tableau: the number of a piece is the step at which the string passes through it. Every geodesic (for a(X) ≤ a(Y)) passes through each cell of R exactly once, so the map is a bijection. The number of tableaux is counted independently here (by filling order ideals of R, without words or the graph) and compared with the number of shortest paths from BFS.

Controls. Start/Pause, ◂ ▸ step, Reset, ← → previous/next tableau in the numbering, Random picks a uniformly random one. Click a thumbnail to select a tableau. Keys: space, ← →.
